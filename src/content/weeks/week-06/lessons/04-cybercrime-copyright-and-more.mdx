## The Cybercrimes Act

The **Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015** was made to fight online crime such as fraud and hacking. But one part, **Section 24**, was often used against journalists and critics over what they posted online.

The original Section 24(1) made it an offence to send a message online that was "grossly offensive". It also covered messages the sender knew to be false, sent for purposes such as "annoyance", "inconvenience" or "insult". The penalty was up to 3 years in prison, a fine of up to ₦7 million, or both.

In March 2022, the ECOWAS Court ruled that Section 24 did not meet Nigeria's human rights obligations, and ordered Nigeria to amend it.

**The 2024 amendment.** The Cybercrimes (Amendment) Act 2024 was signed on 28 February 2024. It narrowed Section 24(1). It now covers messages that are pornographic, or that the sender knows are false and sends to cause a breakdown of law and order or a threat to life.

<Callout type="watch" title="The risk has not gone away">
The Committee to Protect Journalists reported that at least six journalists had been prosecuted under the Act after the 2024 amendment, by November 2025. Other parts of Section 24, and other sections of the Act, are still used in cases against journalists.
</Callout>

What this means for you: be accurate online as well as in your stories. Keep evidence for what you publish. If police contact you about a story, tell your editor at once and ask for legal help.

## Copyright

The **Copyright Act 2022** protects creative work: writing, photos, music, films and more. Though it is called the 2022 Act, it came into force on 17 March 2023. It replaced the old Copyright Act.

This matters a lot for entertainment journalism, where you will want to use photos, clips and songs.

**Fair dealing.** Section 20 allows some uses without permission, "by way of fair dealing" for purposes such as criticism, review or reporting current events. When the use is public, you should credit the title of the work and its author where practicable.

To decide if a use is fair, the Act looks at:

1. the purpose and character of the use
2. the nature of the work
3. how much of the work you use, and how important that part is
4. the effect on the market or value of the work

<Callout type="watch" title="There is no safe number">
You may hear that "30 seconds of a song" or "10% of a work" is always fine. The Act has no such rule. Using a whole photo or a whole music video is less likely to be fair. The safest choices are your own photos, material you have permission to use, and short extracts with credit when you are reviewing or reporting.
</Callout>

## Data protection

The **Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023** controls how personal data is collected and used. It was signed on 12 June 2023.

Journalism gets a **partial** exemption. Section 3(2) says many duties do not apply to processing personal data for journalism in the public interest, "to the extent" those duties conflict with journalism. But some duties still apply, including the basic principles for handling personal data and having a lawful reason to process it.

In practice: collect only the personal data your story needs, keep it secure, and do not publish private details that serve no public interest.

## Other laws to know

**Official Secrets Act.** It is a crime to obtain, pass on or keep "classified matter" without authority. The penalty can be up to 14 years in prison. If someone offers you secret documents, talk to your editor before you do anything with them.

**Contempt of court.** While a case is going on, it is an offence to publish something that could unfairly influence it, or that misrepresents the proceedings. You can report fair and accurate accounts of what happens in open court. Do not say or suggest someone is guilty before the court decides.

**Broadcasting rules.** TV and radio stations must follow the Nigeria Broadcasting Code, set by the National Broadcasting Commission (NBC). The NBC can sanction stations. But courts have ruled that the NBC cannot impose fines by itself, and in April 2026 the Court of Appeal upheld a ruling setting aside NBC fines on 45 stations.
