## Why law matters to journalists

Journalists hold power to account, and people with power do not always like it. Knowing the law helps you report what you have a right to report. It helps you avoid breaking the law by accident. And it helps you see when someone is using the law to scare you.

## What the Constitution says

Two sections of the 1999 Constitution (as amended) matter most to the press.

**Section 39** protects freedom of expression. Section 39(1) says:

> "Every person shall be entitled to freedom of expression, including freedom to hold opinions and to receive and impart ideas and information without interference."

Section 39(2) says anyone may own and run a medium for sharing information, ideas and opinions. But there is a condition for broadcasting. Only the government, or a person or body the President authorises under a law, may run a TV or radio station. That is why broadcasters need a licence.

Section 39(3) sets limits. It allows laws that are "reasonably justifiable in a democratic society", for example to protect information given in confidence, to keep the courts independent, or to regulate broadcasting.

**Section 22** gives the media a duty. It says the press, radio, television and other media "shall at all times be free to uphold the fundamental objectives" in that chapter. They must also be free to "uphold the responsibility and accountability of the Government to the people".

<Callout type="note" title="Can you go to court under Section 22?">
Usually not. Section 22 is in Chapter II of the Constitution, and Section 6(6)(c) says courts generally cannot rule on whether something follows Chapter II. There are exceptions, for example where the Supreme Court has linked a Chapter II goal to another part of the Constitution. Section 39 is different. It is a fundamental right in Chapter IV, and you can go to court to enforce it.
</Callout>

## Freedom has limits, and so does power

Press freedom is not a freedom to say anything. Other laws, like defamation and copyright, still apply, and you will study them in the next lessons.

But the Constitution also limits the government. A law that restricts the press must be reasonably justifiable in a democratic society. Journalists and press freedom groups have gone to court many times to challenge laws and actions they believed went too far.

## Press freedom in Nigeria today

Reporters Without Borders (RSF) ranks countries each year on press freedom. In its 2026 index, Nigeria was **112th of 180 countries**, up from 122nd in 2025. RSF still places Nigeria in its "difficult" category.

The Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ) reported in May 2026 that 91 journalists had been targeted in Nigeria since May 2023, across 13 states and the FCT.

<Callout type="practice">
You do not need to be afraid, but you do need to be careful. Check your facts, keep your notes and recordings, tell your editor about risky stories, and know that press freedom groups exist to help journalists in trouble.
</Callout>
