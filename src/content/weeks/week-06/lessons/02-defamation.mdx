## What defamation is

Defamation is publishing something false that damages a person's reputation. The Criminal Code describes defamatory matter as something likely to expose a person to "hatred, contempt, or ridicule", or to damage them in their profession or trade.

For journalists, it is the legal risk you will meet most often. Every story that says someone did something wrong carries some risk.

There are two kinds:

- **Libel** is defamation in a lasting form, such as print, a website, a broadcast or a social media post.
- **Slander** is defamation in a passing form, such as spoken words.

## Being charged: criminal defamation

In many countries defamation is only a civil matter. **In Nigeria, it can still be a crime.**

- Under the **Criminal Code**, used in southern states, publishing defamatory matter can carry up to one year in prison, or two years if the person knew it was false.
- Under the **Penal Code**, used in northern states and the FCT, defamation can carry up to two years in prison, a fine, or both.

The Supreme Court upheld the Penal Code's defamation offences in 2021.

<Callout type="nigeria" title="Lagos is different, but not free of risk">
The Criminal Law of Lagos State 2011 does not include criminal defamation. The Nigerian Bar Association has said this means libel is not a crime in Lagos. But the same law still has other offences, including publishing false news likely to cause fear or alarm. It is no defence to say you did not know a story was false, unless you took reasonable steps to check it.
</Callout>

People have used criminal complaints against journalists as a way to pressure them, not only to protect a reputation. That is one more reason to get every fact right.

## How to protect yourself

**Check every claim.** Truth is your strongest defence. If you cannot prove it, do not publish it.

**Keep your evidence.** Save notes, recordings, documents and messages. Write down when and where you got them.

**Attribute clearly.** Say who made a claim: "the police said", "according to the court documents". Attribution helps readers, but it does not make a false claim safe to repeat.

**"Allegedly" is not a shield.** Adding the word "allegedly" does not protect you if you repeat a damaging claim with no basis.

**Give a right of reply.** Before you publish a serious claim about someone, contact them, tell them what you plan to say, and include their response. Say if they did not reply.

**Separate fact from opinion.** Make it clear when something is comment, and base comment on facts you have reported.

**Report court cases fairly and accurately.** A fair and accurate report of what happened in open court is protected. Adding your own claims is not.

<Callout type="watch">
Social media posts count. A defamatory tweet, WhatsApp broadcast or TikTok video can lead to a complaint just like a news story. As a journalist, hold your personal posts to the same standard.
</Callout>
