## Start clearly

Always say who you are and who you are working for. On the phone, The News Manual says to do this at once. People have a right to know they are speaking to a journalist.

For now, as a student, say that you are a journalism student and what the interview is for.

## Listen more than you talk

The best interviewers listen closely. Listening is how you spot the follow-up question that leads to the real story.

- **Follow up.** If an answer is vague, ask: "Can you give me an example?" or "What do you mean by that?"
- **Be polite but persistent.** If someone avoids a question, ask it again, perhaps in a different way.
- **Use silence.** Poynter advises: be patient, and do not rush to fill a silence with a new question. People often keep talking and say something important.
- **Watch your reactions.** Nodding can look like agreement. Stay neutral.

<Callout type="note">
Silence works best face to face. The News Manual notes that on the phone a pause does not help in the same way, because the other person cannot see you waiting.
</Callout>

## Before you finish

- **Recap key facts.** Repeat important points back to check you understood.
- **Check spellings and numbers.** Ask how to spell names, and confirm figures, ages and titles.
- **Ask one last question.** The News Manual suggests: "Is there anything else I should know?"
- **Ask how to reach them again.** You may need to check something later.

## Interviewing by phone, messaging, email or video

Face to face is usually best. The News Manual calls phone and online interviews "almost always second-best". But you will often need them.

<Callout type="watch" title="Check who you are talking to">
Scammers can pretend to be officials or celebrities online. Before you use answers from a messaging app or email, confirm the person's identity another way, such as a phone call to a known number.
</Callout>

Media Helping Media gives a simple rule for remote interviews: assume everything is on the record unless you have both agreed otherwise. You will learn what "on the record" means in the next lesson.

## Do not take answers out of context

When you write up the interview, make sure each quote means what the person meant. Cutting a quote so it says something different is a serious breach of trust.
