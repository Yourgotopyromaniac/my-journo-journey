## Why interviews matter

Most news comes from people. An interview is how you find out what someone knows, saw or thinks, in their own words. Good preparation is what separates a useful interview from a wasted one.

## Do your research first

Before you contact anyone, find out what you can:

- Read what has already been reported on the topic.
- Look up the person: their job, what they have said before, what they are responsible for.
- Ask colleagues who know the subject.

The News Manual suggests you then **make a list of the things you need to know**. That list keeps you on track.

## A list, not a script

Your list is a guide, not a script to read out. The News Manual explains why: the next question you ask should depend on the answer you just heard. If you only read out your list, you will miss the most interesting thing the person says.

## Open and closed questions

A **closed question** can be answered with yes or no. It is useful for checking a fact.

An **open question** invites the person to explain, describe or tell a story. It usually begins with what, how or why. Open questions give you better quotes and more detail.

## Ordering your questions

Media Helping Media advises asking your most important question early. The News Manual advises saving difficult or hostile questions until last.

These fit together:

1. Start with a simple question to put the person at ease.
2. Ask your most important question early, while you have their attention.
3. Leave the hardest or most uncomfortable question near the end, so the interview does not end before you get the rest.

## Other preparation tips

- **Do not send your questions in advance.** Media Helping Media advises against it. You can tell the person what the interview is about.
- **Ask one question at a time.** Two questions at once lets the person answer the easier one.
- **Check your tools.** Charge your tablet or phone, clear space for recordings, and bring a notebook and pen as backup.
- **Plan where to meet.** You will learn more about safety in lesson 4.
