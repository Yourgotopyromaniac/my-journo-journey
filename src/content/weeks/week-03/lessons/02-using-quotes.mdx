## Three ways to report what people say

**Direct quotes** use the speaker's exact words inside quotation marks.

**Reported speech**, also called indirect speech or paraphrase, gives the meaning in your own words, without quotation marks. The tense usually moves back: "will" becomes "would", "can" becomes "could".

**Partial quotes** put only a word or phrase in quotation marks. The News Manual says there is seldom any good reason to use them. They often look as if you doubt the speaker, or they break up a sentence awkwardly.

## Never change a quote

Reuters calls quotes "sacrosanct". If something is inside quotation marks, those must be the words the person said.

Guides differ on small grammar fixes. The Associated Press does not correct grammar inside quotes. Reuters says fixing a grammatical error "may be valid", but rewording a phrase is not. The safe habit for a beginner: **if a quote is unclear or badly worded, do not tidy it. Use reported speech instead.**

If you leave words out of the middle of a quote, show it with an ellipsis (...). If you must add a word so the quote makes sense, put it in square brackets: "Some [cattle] have been recovered."

## When to quote and when to paraphrase

A useful rule from journalism teaching: **paraphrase dry facts, but quote emotions, opinions and strong expressions.**

- Paraphrase: The ministry said 2,400 teachers would be employed in the next quarter.
- Quote: "I have waited six years for this job," said one applicant.

Use reported speech when the material is technical, routine or long, or when saying it more simply helps the reader.

## Quotes from press releases and social media

<Callout type="watch">
Quotes in press releases are almost always written by public relations staff, not spoken by the person named. Do not present them as if the person said them to you. Write "the company said in a statement" or "the governor said in a statement".
</Callout>

The same goes for social media. If you quote a post, say where it was posted: "she wrote on X", "he said in an Instagram video". And check that the account is real before you use it. You will learn how in Week 8.

<Callout type="note">
Putting damaging words inside quotation marks does not protect you legally. If you repeat a defamatory claim, it is still your publication. You will learn more about this in Week 6.
</Callout>
