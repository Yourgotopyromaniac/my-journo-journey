## The shape of a news story

Most hard news stories are written in the same shape. It is called the <Term id="inverted-pyramid">inverted pyramid</Term>. "Inverted" means upside down.

The most important information goes at the top. Each paragraph after that is a little less important than the one before. The least important details go at the bottom.

## Why newsrooms use it

**Readers can stop at any point and still get the news.** If someone reads only the first paragraph, they know what happened. If they read three, they know the main details.

**Editors can cut a story from the bottom.** When space or time is short, an editor removes the last paragraphs. The story still makes sense, because nothing important was there.

**It works well online.** People scan on their phones. Search results and social media previews often show only the first lines. The inverted pyramid puts the news where they will see it.

## Building one, step by step

Here is how a made-up story about free school lunches could be built.

1. **Lede.** Students at a secondary school in Ikorodu will get free lunch every school day from Monday, the principal said on Thursday.
2. **Key detail.** A local traders' association is paying for the meals. It raised ₦2 million for the first term.
3. **Why.** The principal said many students were coming to school hungry, and some fell asleep in class.
4. **Quote.** "We could not teach children who had not eaten," she said.
5. **Reaction.** A parent said the lunches would save her family money.
6. **Background.** The school has about 900 students. It is one of several schools in the area where traders have supported pupils.

<Callout type="practice">
To test your order, cover the bottom paragraph with your hand. Does the story still work? Then cover the next one. If the story falls apart early, something important is too low.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="Where did it come from?">
You may read that the inverted pyramid started with the telegraph during the American Civil War, so the most important news got through first if the line was cut. Historians disagree. Studies of old newspapers found that most stories were still told in time order then, and the style became common some years later.
</Callout>

## When not to use it

The inverted pyramid is the standard for breaking and hard news. It is not the only shape.

- **Features and profiles** often start with a scene or a person, then explain why the story matters.
- **Reviews** of films or music build an argument.
- **Explainers** may follow the order of a process, step by step.

You will learn these shapes later in the course. For now, practise the inverted pyramid until it feels natural. Every newsroom will expect you to write in it.
