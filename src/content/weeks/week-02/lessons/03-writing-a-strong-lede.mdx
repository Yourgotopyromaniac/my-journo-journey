## What a lede does

The <Term id="lede">lede</Term> is the opening of a news story. You will also hear it called the "lead" or the "intro". In hard news, it is usually one sentence. It tells the reader the most important thing that happened.

Many readers stop after the first paragraph. Online, many only see the headline and the first line in a preview. So the lede has to carry the news on its own.

A good lede answers the most important of the 5 Ws. It does not need all of them. Pick the facts your reader cares about most, and save the rest for later paragraphs.

<Callout type="note">
This lesson is about ledes for hard news. Features and profiles can start in other ways, such as with a scene or a person. You will learn those in Week 11.
</Callout>

## Weak and strong ledes

<Compare
  items={[
    {
      label: 'Weak',
      text: 'On Tuesday, there was a sitting of the Lagos State House of Assembly where many issues were discussed, including market fees.',
    },
    {
      label: 'Stronger',
      good: true,
      text: 'Traders in Lagos markets could pay less in daily fees from next month, after Lagos State lawmakers voted on Tuesday to cut the charge.',
    },
  ]}
  note="Made-up example for practice."
/>

The weak lede starts with the day and a meeting. The reader has to wait to find out what happened, and it never says what changed.

The stronger lede starts with the people affected and what changes for them. The day and the lawmakers are still there, but later in the sentence.

## Three quick rules

**1. Start with what happened, or what changed.** Not with the date, the time, the venue or who attended.

**2. Keep it short.** Use one sentence. Guides differ on the exact length: some say 20 words or fewer, others 25 to 30. Aim for about 25 words, and try not to go over 30. If your lede needs "and" three times, it is doing too much. Move some facts to the second paragraph.

**3. Use clear, active verbs and specific facts.** "Lawmakers voted to cut the fee" is stronger than "a decision was taken regarding the fee". Use numbers and names where they help: "40 shops", not "many shops".

<Callout type="nigeria">
Many press releases in Nigeria start by listing the important people at an event. Do not copy that order. Readers care about what was decided or what changed, not who sat at the high table.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Compare
  items={[
    { label: 'Starts with time', text: 'At about 10am on Monday, a fire started at a market in Lagos Island.' },
    { label: 'Too much', text: 'A fire that started at about 10am on Monday at a market in Lagos Island, which is one of the busiest in the state and has over 2,000 traders, destroyed 40 shops and caused panic.' },
    { label: 'A question', text: 'What would you do if your shop burned down?' },
    { label: 'Stronger', good: true, text: 'A fire destroyed 40 shops at a Lagos Island market on Monday, but no one was hurt, fire officials said.' },
  ]}
  note="Made-up example for practice."
/>

Notice "fire officials said" at the end of the stronger lede. That is <Term id="attribution">attribution</Term>. It tells the reader where the facts came from. You will learn more about it in Week 3.

## Try it yourself

<Try>
Here are the facts of a made-up story. Write a lede of 30 words or fewer.

- The Lagos State Government announced new BRT bus fares yesterday.
- Fares will rise from ₦500 to ₦700 on most routes.
- The new fares start next Monday.
- Officials said the rise is because of higher fuel and repair costs.
- About 200,000 people use the buses each day.

Then count your words. Did you start with what changed for passengers?
</Try>
