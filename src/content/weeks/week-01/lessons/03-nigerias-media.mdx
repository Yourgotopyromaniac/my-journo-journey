## A big and busy media scene

Nigeria has hundreds of newspapers, TV stations, radio stations and news websites. When you look for a job, you need to know the main types, who runs them, and what each one does well.

The outlets named below are examples, not a full list. All of them were publishing when this lesson was written.

## Newspapers

Nigeria's major newspapers now publish online as well as in print. Examples include The Punch, Vanguard, The Guardian (Nigeria), Daily Trust, THISDAY, The Sun and The Nation.

They cover general news, politics, business, sport and entertainment. Many young journalists start as reporters on these papers.

## Television and radio

**Public broadcasters** are owned by government.

- **NTA** (Nigerian Television Authority) is the national public TV network. It describes itself as the largest TV network in Africa by geographical coverage.
- **Radio Nigeria** (FRCN, the Federal Radio Corporation of Nigeria) describes itself as Africa's largest radio network, with six zonal stations.
- **Voice of Nigeria** is the state-owned international broadcaster, set up in 1961. It broadcasts to audiences outside Nigeria in several languages.

Many state governments also run their own TV and radio stations.

**Private broadcasters** include TV stations such as Channels TV, Arise News and TVC News, and radio stations such as Cool FM, Wazobia FM and Nigeria Info.

## Digital-first outlets

Some outlets started online and have never been mainly in print. They are often called digital-native.

- **Premium Times** was founded in 2011 and is based in Abuja. It publishes general news and investigations.
- **TheCable** and **Daily Post** publish general news online.
- **HumAngle** reports on conflict, security and humanitarian issues.
- **Dataphyte** uses data to explain stories.
- **Pulse Nigeria** and **Legit.ng** are among the most used online news brands. Pulse has strong entertainment and lifestyle coverage.
- **BBC News Pidgin** reports news in Nigerian Pidgin.

## News agency and fact-checkers

The **News Agency of Nigeria (NAN)** is the government's news agency. It was set up by decree in 1976 and began work in October 1978. Like other <Term id="wire-service">wire services</Term>, it supplies stories that other outlets can use.

Nigeria also has independent **fact-checking organisations**:

- **Dubawa**, a project of the Centre for Journalism Innovation and Development (CJID)
- **FactCheckHub**, run by the International Centre for Investigative Reporting (The ICIR)
- **Africa Check**, which has an office in Lagos

<Callout type="practice">
When you read a story, check the outlet's "About us" page. Who owns it? Is it public, private or run by a foundation? Ownership does not make a story right or wrong, but it helps you understand the outlet.
</Callout>
