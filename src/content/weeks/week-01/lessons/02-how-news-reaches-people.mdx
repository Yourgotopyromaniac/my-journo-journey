## The news never stops

Newspapers once came out each morning, and TV news came at fixed times. Now news is published all day and all night, on websites, apps, social media and messaging groups. People call this the 24-hour news cycle.

This changes how journalists work. There is pressure to publish first. But being first is worth nothing if the story is wrong.

## What the research says about Nigeria

Every year, the Reuters Institute at the University of Oxford surveys news users in many countries. It publishes the results as the Digital News Report. Nigeria is one of the countries in it.

In the 2026 report, among the Nigerians surveyed:

- **68%** used WhatsApp for news in the past week. The same share, 68%, used Facebook.
- **54%** used YouTube, **50%** Instagram, and **46%** each used TikTok and X for news.
- TikTok use for news rose by **18 percentage points** in a year, the biggest rise of these platforms.
- **63%** shared news through social media, messaging or email.
- **68%** said they trust most news most of the time. That was joint highest of the 48 countries in the report. The average across all countries was 37%.
- **34%** said news-focused creators, not news outlets, meet most or all of their news needs. That was the highest share of any country surveyed.

The most used news brands offline were BBC News, Channels TV and The Punch. Online, they were BBC News, Pulse.ng and Legit.ng.

<Callout type="watch" title="Read the fine print">
The survey is of English-speaking Nigerians who use the internet, aged 18 to 50. They tend to be younger, richer, better educated and more likely to live in cities than most Nigerians. The report says its findings should not be taken as true for the whole country. When you quote a number from a survey, always check who was asked.
</Callout>

## What this means for you as a journalist

**Your audience is on their phones.** Many people will meet your story as a link in a WhatsApp group or a preview on social media. The first line has to work there.

**False stories spread in the same places.** The same report found that worry about fake news online is highest in Nigeria and Kenya. Journalists who check facts carefully stand out.

**Trust is high, so the duty is high.** When people trust the news, a mistake does more damage. Accuracy protects that trust.

**Creators are part of the picture.** Many young Nigerians get news from individual creators. Some are careful. Some are not. A trained journalist brings verification and fairness, whatever platform they work on.

## Try it yourself

<Try>
For one day, write down every time you come across news.

1. Where did you see it: WhatsApp, TikTok, TV, a website, a friend?
2. Could you tell who first reported it?
3. Did you believe it? Why?

Add the most interesting one to your news diary.
