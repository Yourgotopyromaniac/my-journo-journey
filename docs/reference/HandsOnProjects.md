# Portfolio Projects for Beginner Digital Journalism

---

## Project 1: Personal News Beat Blog

**Project Description:**
Build a focused news blog covering a single topic you're passionate about — local sports, campus life, tech trends, or community events. The final deliverable is a fully functional WordPress or Substack publication with a minimum of **8 original articles**, each featuring a headline, lede paragraph, body copy, at least one image with proper attribution, and relevant tags/categories. The blog should have a consistent visual theme, an About page explaining your editorial focus, and a contact form.

**Skills Gained:**
- Inverted pyramid writing structure
- AP Style formatting basics
- Headline writing and SEO fundamentals
- Content Management System (CMS) navigation (WordPress/Substack)
- Basic image sourcing and copyright compliance (Creative Commons)
- Editorial planning and publishing consistency
- Audience-focused writing voice

**Difficulty Level:** ⭐ Beginner
This project is appropriate because it focuses on the foundational skill of *writing and publishing* without requiring technical coding or complex tools. The platform handles design, and the learner can focus entirely on journalism craft and developing a publishing habit.

**Resume Value:**
Demonstrates that you can produce consistent, publication-ready content independently. Employers see evidence of writing discipline, editorial judgment, and familiarity with industry-standard publishing platforms. It also shows initiative — you didn't wait for an assignment, you created your own beat.

## Project 2: Multimedia Story Package

**Project Description:**
Produce a single, in-depth story on a local issue or community topic presented as a **multimedia web page**. Using a free tool like Adobe Express, Canva for Web, or a simple HTML page, the final deliverable includes: a 600–800 word written article, an **original photo gallery** (minimum 6 photos you took), one **short video or audio clip** (60–90 seconds) you recorded and edited, one **basic data visualization** (a chart or infographic using Datawrapper or Canva), and pull quotes styled for visual impact. Think of it as a mini digital magazine feature.

**Skills Gained:**
- Mobile or DSLR photography basics and photo editing (Lightroom Mobile/Snapseed)
- Audio/video recording and basic editing (CapCut, Audacity, or iMovie)
- Data visualization with Datawrapper or Flourish
- Storytelling across multiple media formats
- Basic web layout and visual hierarchy thinking
- Interview techniques and source attribution
- File compression and web-optimized media

**Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐ Beginner-to-Intermediate
The writing component is familiar from Project 1, but layering in photo, video, and data elements introduces new tools simultaneously. The difficulty is manageable because each media element is short and the tools chosen have gentle learning curves. The challenge is *integration* — making all elements tell one cohesive story.

**Resume Value:**
This is a standout portfolio piece because it mirrors exactly what modern digital newsrooms produce daily. It proves you understand that digital journalism is *not just writing* — it's visual, data-informed, and audience-engaging. Hiring editors at digital outlets specifically look for candidates who can work across formats without needing a full production team.

## Project 3: Data-Driven Investigative Mini-Report

**Project Description:**
Research and publish a short investigative piece built around **publicly available data**. Choose a dataset from sources like data.gov, your city's open data portal, or a government agency report. The final deliverable is a published article (800–1,000 words) that includes: **3 original interactive charts** built in Datawrapper or Flourish, a clear news angle derived from the data ("What does this data *reveal* that people don't know?"), at least **2 on-record sources** you interviewed to provide human context, a methodology note explaining your data sources, and social media promotional copy (one tweet/X post and one LinkedIn caption written for the story).

**Skills Gained:**
- Data journalism fundamentals and source verification
- Spreadsheet basics for data cleaning (Google Sheets)
- Finding and evaluating public records and open datasets
- Building interactive, embeddable visualizations
- Translating numbers into human-interest narratives
- Writing methodology/transparency notes
- Social media distribution strategy for journalism
- FOIA/public records awareness

**Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Intermediate (Ambitious Beginner)
This project pushes beyond comfort zones by requiring data literacy and source development — two skills that separate entry-level journalists from competitive candidates. It's still achievable because the data is publicly available and the tools are beginner-friendly, but it demands critical thinking and journalistic rigor that elevate the work significantly.

**Resume Value:**
Data journalism is one of the **most in-demand skills** in modern newsrooms, and most beginners avoid it entirely — making this project an immediate differentiator. It demonstrates analytical thinking, transparency, and the ability to find original stories rather than just rewrite press releases. This single project can anchor your entire portfolio and is the type of work that gets shared, cited, and noticed by editors at digital-first outlets like The Markup, FiveThirtyEight-style publications, or local investigative teams.
