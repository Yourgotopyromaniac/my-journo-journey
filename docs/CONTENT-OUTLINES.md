# Content outlines: Weeks 7 to 24

Detailed plans for the weeks not yet written. Each week becomes a folder in `src/content/weeks/` using the same shape as Weeks 1 to 6: about 4 lessons, 2 assignments, an 8-question checkpoint quiz, a journal prompt and free resources.

**How to use this file**

1. About three weeks before a week's release date, run a research pass on its "Verify before writing" list. Record every source in `src/content/sources.ts` with the date checked.
2. Write the lessons, assignments and quiz. Follow the plain English rules (REQUIREMENTS.md, section 4.1).
3. Run `npm run build`, read the week in the app, then release.

**Rules that apply to every week**

- Nothing in the "Key points" below is a checked fact yet. Treat each one as a plan until it is verified.
- Label all made-up examples as made up. Do not invent quotes from real people.
- Prefer free resources that work on an Android tablet. Check app availability on the Google Play Store at the time of writing.
- Phase review quizzes (16 to 20 questions) sit in the last week of each phase: Weeks 9, 16, 22 and 24.

# Phase 2: Truth and verification

Goal: Princess can check a claim, find public information, and use numbers without being misled.

## Week 7: Research and records

**By the end of the week she can**
- Tell primary sources from secondary sources, and say when each is useful.
- Use the SIFT method to judge a website or claim.
- Use advanced search operators to find documents.
- Name at least five places to find public records about Nigeria.

**Lessons**
1. **Primary and secondary sources.** What each is. Why a court judgment beats a news report about it. Examples: a budget document versus a story about the budget.
2. **SIFT: a quick way to judge information.** Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims to the original. Work through one made-up WhatsApp forward.
3. **Searching like a journalist.** Google search operators: quotes, `site:`, `filetype:pdf`, minus sign, date tools. Search on a tablet. Google reverse image search as a preview of Week 8.
4. **Public records in Nigeria.** Where to look: official gazettes, National Bureau of Statistics reports, budget documents, Corporate Affairs Commission public search, court judgments, INEC data, National Assembly bills. What each can tell you, and what it cannot.

**Assignments**
- **Research trail (75 min).** Pick a local issue (for example, a road project in her area). Find three primary sources and three secondary sources. For each, note what it says, who made it and how reliable it is. Deliverable: a research log in Google Docs.
- **Search drill (45 min).** Five search tasks using operators, such as "find a PDF report from a Nigerian government agency about X". Record the search terms used. Deliverable: search log.

**Quiz focus:** primary versus secondary; SIFT steps (order question); choosing the right operator; which record answers which question.

**Journal prompt:** Think of something you once believed because many people shared it. How would SIFT have helped?

**Verify before writing**
- SIFT: original source (Mike Caulfield) and a free explainer page.
- Current Google search operators that still work (some were removed).
- CAC public search: URL, what is free, what needs payment.
- NBS, budget office, National Assembly and INEC data portals: current URLs.
- NigeriaLII or another free source of Nigerian court judgments.

**Resources to consider:** Google News Initiative search lessons; a free SIFT explainer; NBS website tour.

## Week 8: Fact-checking

**By the end of the week she can**
- Choose which claims are worth checking.
- Check a claim step by step and give a clear verdict.
- Check where an image or video came from.
- Spot common signs of AI-generated or edited media.

**Lessons**
1. **What fact-checkers do.** Checkable versus not checkable claims (facts versus opinions and predictions). How Nigerian fact-checkers choose claims. Ratings such as true, misleading and false.
2. **Checking a claim, step by step.** Find the original claim, find the evidence, ask the person who made it, ask experts, write the verdict. Show the working.
3. **Images and videos.** Reverse image search, checking dates and locations, old photos shared as new, cropped images. Tools that work on a tablet.
4. **Deepfakes, AI images and cheap fakes.** What they are. Signs to look for, and why these signs are getting less reliable. Why to check the source before the pixels.

**Assignments**
- **Three fact-checks (90 min).** Choose three claims from social media or public figures. Write a 150 to 250 word fact-check for each, with sources and a verdict. Deliverable: Google Doc.
- **Image detective (40 min).** Given three images (links to real, well-documented cases of misused photos), trace each to its origin. Deliverable: short notes with links.

**Quiz focus:** checkable claims; verification steps; what reverse image search can and cannot show; recognising a misleading (not false) claim.

**Toolkit:** add the **Verification checklist**.

**Verify before writing**
- Dubawa, FactCheckHub and Africa Check methods pages (how they rate claims).
- Free reverse image search tools usable on Android Chrome: Google Lens, TinEye, others. Check InVID/WeVerify still offers a free option and whether it works on tablets.
- Two or three documented cases of old images misused in Nigerian news or social media, with fact-check links.
- Current, reputable guidance on spotting AI-generated images and video.

**Resources to consider:** academy.AFRICA "Introduction to Fact-checking" (free); Google News Initiative Verification lessons.

## Week 9: Numbers for journalists (+ Phase 2 review quiz)

**By the end of the week she can**
- Work out percentages and percentage change, and explain the difference between a percentage and a percentage point.
- Choose between mean and median.
- Explain inflation in plain words using official figures.
- Spot a misleading chart or statistic.

**Lessons**
1. **Percentages without fear.** Percentage, percentage change, percentage points. Worked examples in naira. Using a calculator or Google Sheets on a tablet.
2. **Averages and what they hide.** Mean versus median, with a salary example. Why "average" can mislead.
3. **Inflation and official statistics.** What the inflation rate means, where NBS publishes it, why a lower inflation rate does not mean prices fell. Sample sizes and margins of error in polls, briefly.
4. **Misleading charts and numbers.** Cut-off axes, cherry-picked dates, big numbers with no context ("₦10 billion" versus per person), correlation versus cause.

**Assignments**
- **Spot the error (60 min).** Find five news stories that use numbers. For each: is the source named, is the number used correctly, is anything missing? Deliverable: critique table.
- **Numbers practice (45 min).** A made-up data set (for example, market prices over six months). Work out changes, averages and one sentence a reporter could write. Deliverable: Google Sheet plus five sentences.

**Quiz focus:** calculations (single choice); percentage versus percentage points; mean versus median; misleading chart examples.

**Phase 2 review quiz:** 16 to 20 questions across Weeks 7 to 9.

**Toolkit:** add a **Numbers checklist**.

**Verify before writing**
- The latest NBS inflation release: headline figure, month, and any change in method (the NBS rebased the CPI; confirm details and date).
- A free, reputable guide to statistics for journalists (for example, from Media Helping Media, the Royal Statistical Society, or Full Fact).
- Examples of misleading charts from a reputable media-literacy source.

**Resources to consider:** a free numeracy-for-journalists guide; Google Sheets basics on Android.

# Phase 3: Digital craft

Goal: Princess can produce web-ready stories, features, photos, charts, short video and audio on a tablet, and use AI tools with care.

## Week 10: Writing for the web

**By the end of the week she can**
- Format a story for reading on a phone.
- Write headlines for search and for social media without clickbait.
- Explain basic SEO and how it affects headlines and first paragraphs.
- Publish a formatted story in a CMS (private practice site).

**Lessons**
1. **How people read online.** Scanning, short paragraphs, subheadings, links, previews on WhatsApp and social media.
2. **Headlines that work online.** Clear, specific and honest. SEO headline versus social headline. Clickbait and why it harms trust.
3. **SEO basics for journalists.** Search intent, keywords in headlines and first paragraphs, meta descriptions, internal links. Using Google Trends.
4. **Publishing in a CMS.** What a CMS is. Posts, categories, tags, featured images, alt text, slugs. Practice on a private WordPress.com site.

**Assignments**
- **Web explainer (90 min).** A 400 to 500 word explainer on a topic she knows, formatted for the web, published privately in WordPress.com. Deliverable: private link and screenshot.
- **Headline workshop (40 min).** Three headlines (search, social, print style) for five stories. Deliverable: table with a short reason for each.

**Quiz focus:** web formatting choices; spotting clickbait; what a meta description and alt text are; SEO myths.

**Toolkit:** add a **Web publishing checklist**.

**Verify before writing**
- WordPress.com free plan: can posts or the whole site be private, and does the Android app support it?
- Google Trends works for Nigeria and on a tablet.
- A current, reputable SEO guide for newsrooms (for example, Google Search Central documentation for news publishers).

**Resources to consider:** Google Search Central news guidance; Google News Initiative SEO lessons.

## Week 11: Features and profiles

**By the end of the week she can**
- Write a nut graf that says why a story matters.
- Open a feature with a scene, a person or a surprising detail.
- Structure a 600 to 800 word profile.
- Edit her own work before sending it.

**Lessons**
1. **What makes a feature different.** Features versus hard news. Types: profile, human interest, explainer, trend, review (reviews come in Week 21).
2. **Openings and the nut graf.** Scene-setting with the senses, anecdotal intros, delayed ledes, and the nut graf that follows.
3. **Writing about people.** Researching a person, interviewing for detail, showing not telling, fair and accurate description.
4. **Self-editing.** Cutting clutter, checking facts and names, reading aloud, the "cover the paragraph" test from Week 2 in reverse.

**Assignments**
- **Nut graf practice (40 min).** Find three features; mark their nut grafs. Write nut grafs for three hard news stories from her diary.
- **Profile (120 min).** Interview someone in her community and write a 600 to 800 word profile with a scene opening, nut graf and at least three quotes. Deliverable: Google Doc.

**Quiz focus:** identifying a nut graf; choosing a feature opening; feature versus news structure; self-editing choices.

**Verify before writing**
- A free, reputable guide to feature writing (for example, Media Helping Media, The News Manual, Poynter free articles).
- Two or three strong, free-to-read Nigerian features or profiles to use as examples, with links (check they are not paywalled).

**Resources to consider:** a free feature-writing guide; one Nigerian long-form outlet or series to read.

## Week 12: Social media and safety

**By the end of the week she can**
- Use social media to find story ideas and sources, and check them.
- Set up private, secure accounts ready for later professional use.
- Protect her devices and sources with basic digital security.
- Recognise online harassment and know what to do about it.

**Lessons**
1. **Social media as a reporting tool.** Finding stories, lists and search, following official accounts, verifying accounts and posts (link back to Week 8).
2. **Your professional presence (for later).** What editors look for. Bio basics. Keeping accounts private until she is ready, as agreed. Posting rules for journalists.
3. **Digital security basics.** Strong passwords and a password manager, two-factor authentication, screen locks, Signal for sensitive chats, photo metadata, backups.
4. **Online harassment.** What it can look like, especially for women journalists. Documenting, blocking, reporting, and asking for help. Where Nigerian journalists can get support.

**Assignments**
- **Social story hunt (60 min).** Find three possible stories on social media. For each: the angle, two sources to contact, and how to verify the first claim.
- **Security setup (45 min).** Work through the security checklist on her own phone, tablet and accounts. Deliverable: completed checklist (no passwords written down).

**Quiz focus:** verifying accounts; two-factor authentication; metadata in photos; safe responses to harassment.

**Toolkit:** add the **Digital security checklist**.

**Verify before writing**
- Current free security guides for journalists (for example, CPJ Digital Safety Kit, Freedom of the Press Foundation, Security Planner).
- Guidance on online harassment of women journalists (for example, IWMF, International Women's Media Foundation; UNESCO research), and any Nigerian support organisations.
- How to remove location data from photos on Android (current steps).
- Platform reporting tools on X, Instagram, TikTok and Facebook (they change often).

**Resources to consider:** CPJ Digital Safety Kit; an IWMF online harassment guide.

## Week 13: Photos and charts

**By the end of the week she can**
- Take clear news photos on a tablet or phone using basic composition.
- Edit photos within ethical limits.
- Write accurate, complete captions.
- Make a simple, honest chart.

**Lessons**
1. **News photos on a phone or tablet.** Light, framing, rule of thirds, getting close, shooting a sequence. Asking permission and photographing people fairly.
2. **Ethical editing and captions.** What is allowed (crop, brightness) and what is not (adding or removing content). Caption rules: who, what, where, when, credit.
3. **Using images you did not take.** Copyright (link back to Week 6), Creative Commons, credits, asking permission.
4. **Simple charts.** Choosing bar, line or table. Honest axes. Datawrapper basics on a tablet browser, or Google Sheets charts.

**Assignments**
- **Photo story (90 min).** Five photos about one subject in her community, with captions and a 100-word introduction.
- **One honest chart (45 min).** Use the Week 9 data or a public data set to make one chart with a title, source and clear labels.

**Quiz focus:** composition choices; allowed edits; caption completeness; choosing a chart type; misleading axes.

**Verify before writing**
- Snapseed and other free photo editors still on the Google Play Store.
- Datawrapper free plan and whether it works well in a tablet browser.
- Creative Commons licence types and a free, reputable explainer.
- A published photojournalism ethics code (for example, NPPA) with a free link.

**Resources to consider:** Datawrapper Academy; a free mobile photography guide for journalists.

## Week 15: Audio and AI tools

**By the end of the week she can**
- Record clean audio on a tablet.
- Write for the ear.
- Produce a 2-minute audio report.
- Use AI tools for journalism tasks with care, and explain the risks.

**Lessons**
1. **Recording audio.** Quiet spaces, mic distance, recording apps, checking levels.
2. **Writing for the ear.** Short sentences, one idea at a time, saying numbers simply, repeating key facts.
3. **AI tools in the newsroom.** What they can help with (transcription, summaries, translation, idea lists) and where they fail (made-up facts and quotes, bias).
4. **Rules for using AI.** Never publish AI output without checking. Be open about AI use. Protect sources' data. Examples of newsroom AI policies.

**Assignments**
- **Two-minute audio report (90 min).** Script, record and edit a short report on a local story.
- **AI tool test (45 min).** Use a free AI assistant for three tasks (interview questions, summary, headlines). Check every output. Note errors. Deliverable: short evaluation.

**Quiz focus:** writing for the ear; AI limitations; when AI use must be disclosed; checking AI transcription.

**Note:** This week follows the Christmas flex week. Keep it realistic for someone coming back from a break.

**Verify before writing**
- Free Android audio recording and editing apps.
- Two or three published newsroom AI policies (for example, from a major outlet and, if available, a Nigerian outlet).
- Current, reputable guidance on AI in journalism (for example, from the Reuters Institute or Poynter).

**Resources to consider:** a newsroom AI policy; a free guide to writing for radio.

# Phase 4: Your two beats

Goal: Princess understands how politics and entertainment work in Nigeria and can report on both accurately and fairly.

## Week 17: How Nigeria is governed

**By the end of the week she can**
- Explain the three arms and three tiers of government.
- Explain how a bill becomes law.
- Say what key institutions do, such as INEC, CBN, EFCC and the courts.
- Find primary documents: bills, acts, budgets and judgments.

**Lessons**
1. **The constitution and the arms of government.** Executive, legislature, judiciary. Separation of powers. Federal, state and local government.
2. **How laws are made.** Bills in the National Assembly and state Houses of Assembly, readings, committees, assent, overriding a veto.
3. **Key institutions.** INEC, CBN, EFCC, ICPC, the police, the courts, the Code of Conduct Bureau. What each does and whom it answers to.
4. **Finding the documents.** Where bills, acts, budgets and court judgments are published. Reading a bill without panic.

**Assignments**
- **Explainer on a current bill or policy (90 min).** 500 words, with links to the primary document.
- **Government map (45 min).** A one-page map of who governs her state and local government area, and how to contact them.

**Quiz focus:** arms and tiers; bill stages (order question); which institution does what; primary documents.

**Toolkit:** add a **Covering government checklist**.

**Verify before writing**
- Constitution sections on the legislature and executive; process for a bill (use the constitution text and National Assembly sources).
- Current mandates of INEC, CBN, EFCC and ICPC from their own websites.
- Number of states and local government areas, and any changes (for example, state creation debates).
- Free sources of Nigerian laws and bills (PLAC, NigeriaLII, National Assembly).
- Any constitutional amendments passed since 2023.

**Resources to consider:** PLAC explainers; National Assembly website.

## Week 18: Covering elections

**By the end of the week she can**
- Explain the election timetable and the roles of INEC and parties.
- Report polls and results responsibly.
- Fact-check claims made by politicians.
- Report on ethnicity, religion and hate speech without making things worse.

**Lessons**
1. **How elections work in Nigeria.** The Electoral Act, INEC's timetable, primaries, campaigns, voting, collation, results, tribunals.
2. **Covering campaigns and polls.** Rallies, manifestos, polls and their limits, avoiding horse-race-only coverage.
3. **Election day and results.** Reporting from polling units, official results versus claims, safety.
4. **Fair and safe reporting.** Hate speech, ethnic and religious sensitivity, misinformation around elections, fact-checking politicians.

**Assignments**
- **Campaign claim fact-check (75 min).** Fact-check one claim from the 2027 campaign.
- **Election coverage plan (60 min).** A plan for covering election day in one local government area: sources, safety, verification, publishing.

**Quiz focus:** election stages; INEC's role; reporting polls; hate speech; results claims.

**Time-sensitive: research this close to release.** This week runs in mid to late January 2027, during the build-up to the 2027 general elections.

**Verify before writing**
- INEC's official timetable for the 2027 general elections (dates may change; use INEC's own release).
- The Electoral Act in force (check for any 2025 or 2026 amendments) and key provisions on results transmission and campaign periods.
- Rules for journalists on election day (accreditation).
- Election safety guidance for journalists (CPJ, IPC).
- Reputable Nigerian election fact-checking projects running for 2027.

**Resources to consider:** INEC's website; an election reporting guide for Nigeria; a CPJ election safety note.

**Lessons**
1. **How newsrooms hire.** Internships, graduate trainee schemes, freelance-to-staff routes, writing tests. What editors look for.
2. **CV and cover letter.** One to two pages, clips first, tailored to the outlet.
3. **Pitching.** Subject line, the story in one paragraph, why now, why you. Following up politely.
4. **Interviews and your next six months.** Common questions, a writing test practice, a 6-month plan.

**Assignments**
- **CV and cover letter (120 min).** For a real or realistic role.
- **Writing test practice (60 min).** Timed: a press release turned into a news story in 30 minutes, then edited.
- **Six-month plan (30 min).**

**Final review quiz:** 16 to 20 questions across the whole course.
