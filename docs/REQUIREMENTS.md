# My Journo Journey — Requirements

**Status:** Approved · **Last updated:** 13 Sep 2026 · **Owner/maintainer:** Abiola Awoyemi

---

## 1. Overview

My Journo Journey is a personal learning companion web app that guides **Princess** through a structured, 6-month self-study roadmap in digital journalism. It teaches general journalism foundations first, then specialises in **politics** and **entertainment**, and ends with preparation for a **staff newsroom job**.

The app is the roadmap, the classroom and the study partner in one place: short lessons, practical assignments, quizzes after every checkpoint, a journal and news diary, and gentle weekly pacing.

### 1.1 Learner profile

| | |
|---|---|
| **Learner** | Princess |
| **Background** | Final-year Mass Communication student, University of Lagos; complete beginner in practice |
| **Location** | Lagos, Nigeria |
| **Goal** | Primary: staff job in a newsroom. Secondary: freelance / other routes |
| **Specialisms** | Politics and entertainment (after general foundations) |
| **Time** | 8 hours per week |
| **Device** | Android tablet (primary); app must work on all screen sizes |
| **Online presence** | None yet; work stays private until she chooses to go public |

### 1.2 Success criteria

By the end of the programme Princess should:

1. Have completed 24 content weeks and passed every checkpoint quiz.
2. Have a private body of work (in Google Docs) including at least: hard news stories, an interview write-up, a fact-check, a feature/profile, a data-led piece, a short video, a politics piece and an entertainment piece.
3. Understand Nigerian media ethics and law well enough to apply a pre-publication checklist to her own work.
4. Have a CV, cover letter, pitch and curated set of 6–8 best pieces ready for staff job applications.

## 3. Curriculum

### 3.1 Principles

- **Foundations before specialism.** Weeks 1–16 are general; beats start in Week 17.
- **Ethics and law early and throughout**, not bolted on at the end.
- **Nigerian context first.** Nigerian law, institutions, outlets, examples and British English spelling; global resources where they are the best available.
- **Achievable on an Android tablet** with free apps and web tools.
- **Free resources only.**
- **Learning by doing.** Every week produces a piece of work.
- **Realistic for 8 hours/week:** ≈3h lessons · ≈3h assignments · ≈1h reading + news diary · ≈1h quiz, review and journal.

### 3.2 Schedule

- **Week 1 starts Monday 14 September 2026.** (Moved forward one week from 21 September on 13 Sep 2026.)
- **Flex week 1** is fixed over Christmas (21–27 Dec 2026), between Weeks 14 and 15.
- **Flex week 2** floats: by default it sits at the end (8–14 Mar 2027), but Princess can take it at any point (e.g. exams). Taking it early shifts all later weeks by one.
- **Programme ends 14 March 2027.**

## 4. Content standards

1. **Accuracy.** Every factual claim in a lesson is supported by a cited source. No invented statistics, quotes or examples. Where facts may change (laws, dates, institutions), cite the primary source.
2. **Sources.** Prefer primary sources (legislation, official bodies) and established organisations. Each lesson ends with a "Sources" list.
3. **External resources are free**, work on an Android tablet (browser or Android app), and carry a **"last verified" date**. Links are checked before release.
4. **Language.** Clear, basic English with British spelling. See Section 4.1.
5. **Lesson length.** 10–15 minutes each; 3–5 lessons per week. Hands-on tool skills link out to reputable free tutorials rather than duplicating them.
6. **Examples** use Nigerian contexts where possible and never misrepresent real people. Sensitive topics are handled with care.
7. **Assignments** each have: a brief, estimated time, a deliverable, a self-assessment checklist, and guidance on where to save the work (Google Docs/Drive).
8. **Release cadence.** Content is always published at least two weeks ahead of Princess's schedule.

### 4.1 Plain English rules

All lessons, quizzes, assignments and app text follow these rules.

**Write like a good tutor talking to her**
- Use short sentences. Aim for under 20 words.
- Use everyday words. If a simpler word works, use it ("use", not "utilise"; "help", not "facilitate").
- Talk to her directly as "you".
- Say a thing once. Do not repeat it in different words.
- Explain with a concrete example, ideally a Nigerian one, not an abstract idea.
- Define a journalism term the first time it appears, and link it to the glossary.
- Aim for a reading level of about grade 8.

**Avoid AI-style writing**
- No filler openings or endings: "Let's dive in", "In today's fast-paced digital world", "In conclusion", "Happy learning!"
- No inflated words: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, navigate (unless literal), journey (except the app name), embark, unlock, empower, elevate, leverage, robust, seamless, foster, pivotal, crucial, nuanced, testament, vibrant, game-changer.
- No "It's not just X, it's Y" or "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- No lists of three used only for rhythm.
- No rhetorical questions used as headings or hooks.
- No em dashes. Use a full stop, comma or brackets.
- No over-praise: "Amazing!", "You're crushing it!". Use plain encouragement: "Well done. You passed."
- No hedging filler: "It's worth noting that", "Arguably", "It's important to remember".
- Bold only for key terms, not for emphasis on every line. No emoji in lesson text.

**App text (buttons, labels, messages)**
- Short and literal: "Start quiz", "Mark as done", "You're a little behind. Your flex week can help."

**Checking**
- A content lint script runs at build time and flags banned words, em dashes and very long sentences.
- Every lesson is read through against these rules before release.

## 5. Functional requirements

Priority: **M** = must have for launch · **S** = should have · **C** = could have

### 5.1 Onboarding — M
- First-run welcome personalised to Princess, explaining how the programme works (weekly rhythm, quizzes, flex weeks, privacy).
- Prompt to **install the app** to the home screen and explain backups.
- Confirm start date (set in src/content/course.ts; currently 14 Sep 2026).

### 5.2 Dashboard — M
- Greeting with her name and the current programme week and dates.
- **This week's goal** card: progress through lessons, assignments, checkpoint quiz and news diary entries.
- **Continue where you left off.**
- Review questions due (from spaced review).
- Phase progress overview and what's coming next.
- Pacing status in supportive language ("On track", "You're a little behind. Your flex week can help.").

### 5.3 Roadmap — M
- Timeline of all phases and weeks with real calendar dates, status (not started / in progress / complete) and flex weeks.
- **Phase locks (added 13 Sep 2026):** Phase 1 is open from the start. Each later phase opens only when every week of the phase before it is complete, including its review quiz. Locked weeks show a lock and a friendly toast, and cannot be opened by link or search. Within an open phase, all weeks stay open (soft gating), and the recommended next step is highlighted.
- Take a flex week (shifts later dates; confirmation required; reversible).

### 5.4 Week (module) page — M
- Overview, learning objectives (as a checklist), estimated time.
- Ordered lessons, assignments, checkpoint quiz and journal prompt.
- Week completion: all lessons read, assignments marked done, checkpoint quiz passed.

### 5.5 Lesson reader — M
- Comfortable long-form reading layout; adjustable text size; distraction-free mode.
- Rich content: headings, callouts ("In practice", "Nigerian context", "Watch out"), examples, pull quotes, images, embedded external resources/links, glossary term tooltips.
- Sources list; estimated reading time; mark as complete; next/previous navigation.
- Remembers reading position.

### 5.6 Assignments — M
- Brief, deliverable, estimated time, self-assessment checklist.
- Status: not started / in progress / done.
- Optional note and link to her Google Doc.

### 5.7 Quizzes — M
- **Checkpoint quiz** at the end of every week (5–8 questions).
- **Phase review quiz** at the end of each phase (15–20 questions, drawing on the whole phase).
- Question types: multiple choice (single and multi-select), true/false, scenario ("What should you do?"), spot the error (choose the flawed element in a headline/lede), ordering (with accessible up/down controls, not drag-only).
- Explanation shown for every answer, with a link back to the relevant lesson section.
- Pass mark 75%; unlimited retries; question and option order shuffled on retry.
- Attempt history with best score.

### 5.8 Spaced review — S
- Missed questions enter a review queue and resurface after ~2, ~7 and ~21 days until answered correctly.
- Short review sessions launched from the dashboard.

### 5.9 Journal — M
- Weekly reflection prompts tied to the week's module, plus free entries.
- List and search of past entries.

### 5.10 News diary — M
- Quick entry: headline, outlet, link (optional), why it caught her attention, news values it shows (tags).
- Gentle weekly target (default 5 entries).

### 5.11 Toolkit & glossary — S
- Glossary of journalism terms, linked from lessons.
- Quick-reference cards: style basics, ethics checklist, pre-publication legal checklist, verification checklist, interview checklist.
- Resource library: all external resources, filterable by phase/topic/type.

### 5.12 Milestones — S
- Warm, private celebration at the end of each phase and of the programme (e.g. a phase-complete card).

### 5.13 Search — C
- Search across lessons, glossary and toolkit.

### 5.14 Settings & data — M
- Theme (system/light/dark), text size, reduced motion respect.
- Start date and flex week management.
- **Export backup** (JSON file) and **import backup**, with validation and confirmation.
- Reset progress (with strong confirmation).

## 6. UX & design requirements

- **Tablet-first, responsive for all screens:** Android tablet in portrait and landscape is the primary target; phone and desktop fully supported.
- **Touch-friendly:** interactive targets ≥ 48×48 dp.
- **Calm and encouraging tone:** no guilt-driven messaging; progress framed positively.
- **Focus:** one clear primary action per screen; minimal navigation depth; no distracting notifications.
- **Designed states:** empty, loading, error, offline and first-run states for every screen.
- **Design system:** colour, typography, spacing, radius and elevation tokens; consistent components.
- **Design direction:** to be chosen from mockups of the proposed directions:
  1. *The Broadsheet* — editorial, newspaper-inspired
  2. *Lagos Studio* — warm, vibrant, personal
  3. *Press Pass* — calm, minimal, focused
  (or a hybrid)

## 7. Non-functional requirements

| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Accessibility** | WCAG 2.2 AA; keyboard and screen reader support; visible focus; `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; sufficient contrast in both themes |
| **Performance** | Lighthouse ≥ 90 (performance, accessibility, best practices); LCP < 2.5s on a mid-range Android device over 4G; route-level code splitting; optimised images |
| **Offline / PWA** | Installable; app shell and all lesson text cached for offline use; images cached on first view; offline indicator |
| **Data persistence** | Local-only (IndexedDB); request persistent storage; versioned data schema with migrations; export/import backup; backup reminder at intervals |
| **Browser support** | Primary: Chrome on Android. Also latest versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari |
| **Privacy** | No accounts, no analytics, no third-party trackers; nothing leaves the device except external links she opens |
| **Maintainability** | Content in version-controlled files, checked at build time; TypeScript throughout; linting and formatting enforced |
